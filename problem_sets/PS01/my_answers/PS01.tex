	\section*{Question 1: Education}

A school counselor was curious about the average of IQ of the students in her school and took a random sample of 25 students' IQ scores. The following is the data set:\\
\vspace{.5cm}

\lstinputlisting[language=R, firstline=39, lastline=39]{PS01.R}  

\vspace{1cm}

\begin{enumerate}
	\item Find a 90\% confidence interval for the average student IQ in the school (As a hint, you first need the mean and SD to create a CI):\\
	
	\lstinputlisting[language=R, firstline=40, lastline=80]{PS01.R}  
	
	\noindent The sample mean is 98.44, the sample standard deviation is 13.09, and the sample size is 25. The confidence interval is calculated as the sample mean $\pm$ the critical value $\times$ the standard error. Because the population standard deviation is unknown and has to be estimated from the sample (and the sample is small), I use the t-distribution with df=n-1=24 instead of the normal distribution. The t-distribution has thicker tails, so it gives a slightly wider interval, which accounts for the extra uncertainty from estimating the SD.
	
	\noindent For a 90\% confidence interval, alpha=0.10, so I leave 5\% in each tail and the t value is 1.71. The standard error is 2.619 (13.09/$\sqrt{25}$), and the margin of error is 1.71 $\times$ 2.619 = 4.48. Therefore, the confidence interval is 98.44 $\pm$ 4.48, or 93.96 to 102.92. Using the t.test() function gives the same interval.
	
	\noindent So, I am 90\% confident that the mean IQ of students at this school lies between 93.96 and 102.92. This means that if we took many random samples of 25 students and built an interval this way each time, about 90\% of those intervals would contain the true mean IQ.
	
	\item Next, the school counselor was curious  whether  the average student IQ in her school is higher than the average IQ score (100) among all the schools in the country.\\ 
	
	\noindent Using the same sample, conduct the appropriate hypothesis test with $\alpha=0.05$.
	
	\lstinputlisting[language=R, firstline=82, lastline=101]{PS01.R}  
	
	\noindent This is a one-sided t-test because the counselor wants to know whether the average student IQ in her school is \textit{higher} than 100, which is a specific direction, not just whether it is different from 100. The null hypothesis is that average student IQ in her school = 100 ($H_0: \mu = 100$), and the alternative hypothesis is that it's greater than 100 ($H_A: \mu > 100$). I use a t-test for the same reason as above: the population SD is unknown and estimated from the sample.
	
	\noindent The test statistic is taken by comparing the sample mean to 100 and then dividing by standard error: (98.44 - 100)/2.619 = -0.596. Because $H_A$ is $\mu > 100$, the p-value is the area in the upper tail of the t-distribution (when lower.tail = FALSE). Using 24 degrees of freedom, the one-sided p-value is 0.722. 
	
	\noindent Since p=0.722 is greater than alpha=0.05, I fail to reject the null hypothesis. There is insufficient evidence at the 5\% significance level to conclude that the mean IQ of students at this school is greater than 100. In addition, the sample mean (98.44) is below 100, so the sample doesn't point in the direction of the alternative hypothesis at all.
	
\end{enumerate}
